Web Performance

Development DEV-06

Detailed description

Web performance measures a site’s speed: how long does it take to display and to respond? A slow site drives visitors away, since they hardly wait more than a few seconds, and Google ranks it lower. On mobile, with an average connection, every second counts even more.

The most frequent causes of slowness are images that are too heavy, too many scripts, weak hosting or too many fonts. The remedies are well known: compress images (and use modern formats like WebP), load images only when they become visible (lazy loading), lighten the code, use the cache (keeping in memory what does not change) and a content delivery network (CDN). You measure with free tools such as PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse. And a light site uses less energy: it is also an eco-design gesture.

Examples of use cases

  • Compressing the images of a home page to cut its loading time in half.
  • Measuring speed before and after an optimization to prove the gain to the client.
  • Enabling deferred loading for photos located at the bottom of the page.

Vocabulary to know

Loading time
The time before the page is usable.
Cache
A copy kept to avoid reloading everything at each visit.
CDN
A network of servers spread around the world that brings content closer to the visitor.
