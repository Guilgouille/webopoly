Print Materials

Graphic Design DES-06

Detailed description

“Print” materials are printed documents: business cards, flyers, posters, brochures, roll-up banners (tall vertical banners), packaging. They extend the brand’s identity into the physical world. But preparing a file for printing has nothing to do with creating an image for a screen.

A screen makes colors with light (RGB), while a printer uses cyan, magenta, yellow and black inks (CMYK): colors may look a little less vivid on paper. You also need high resolution (about 300 dots per inch), a bleed (a few millimeters of image extending beyond the final size to avoid a white edge when trimming) and safety margins so that text is not cut off. Before launching a large print run, you ask for a proof (called a BAT, bon à tirer, in French) to approve the final preview.

Examples of use cases

  • Creating business cards and a roll-up banner for a trade show.
  • Designing the opening flyer of a restaurant, with the bleed planned for trimming.
  • Approving the proof before printing 1,000 copies, to catch a typo or a disappointing color.

Vocabulary to know

CMYK
The color system used for printing.
Bleed
The margin of image that extends beyond the final size.
Proof (BAT)
The sample you approve before printing.
