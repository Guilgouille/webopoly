Newsletter

Communication COM-02

Detailed description

A newsletter is an email sent regularly to a list of people who agreed to receive it. Its great strength compared with social networks: on a network, the platform owns the audience and can change its rules overnight. An email list, on the other hand, belongs to the agency. It is a bit like the difference between renting a stall in a market and owning your own shop.

A good newsletter gives the reader something useful (a tip, some news, an example), not just advertising. It rests on three elements: a short subject line that makes people want to open the email, clear content, and a call to action (a button, for example “Read the article”). Legally, you need the recipients’ consent and a visible unsubscribe link in every email.

Examples of use cases

  • An agency sends “3 web trends of the month” every month to its clients and prospects.
  • An online shop tells its subscribers about a private sale before everyone else.
  • An agency shares a fresh case study with people who have already asked for a quote.

Vocabulary to know

Open rate
The percentage of recipients who open the email.
GDPR (RGPD in French)
The European regulation that protects personal data, including email addresses.
CTA (call to action)
The button or link that invites people to act.
