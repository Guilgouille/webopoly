Iconography

Graphic Design DES-05

Detailed description

Iconography covers a brand’s icons (small pictograms like the magnifying glass or the shopping cart) and illustrations. They help people understand faster than text alone: the magnifying glass means “search”, the envelope means “message”. They make a website clearer and more recognizable.

Its golden rule is unity of style: the same stroke thickness, the same corners (rounded or square), the same choice between filled icons or simple outlines. Mixing styles gives a messy result. An icon must also remain understandable: if its meaning is not obvious, you add a label. You can pick from free icon libraries or have custom ones drawn to get a unique identity.

Examples of use cases

  • A set of 30 icons in the same style for all the pages of a services website.
  • Replacing an ambiguous icon with a version accompanied by the word “Favorites”.
  • Custom illustrations on an application’s home page to give it character.

Vocabulary to know

Pictogram
A simple drawing that represents an idea or an action.
Outline / filled style
An icon drawn by its contour or as a filled shape.
SVG
A vector format widely used for icons on the web.
