Content Strategy

Communication COM-04

Detailed description

A content strategy is a plan that answers five questions: why publish (the goal: be known, get quotes, build loyalty), for whom (the target), what (topics and formats: article, video, post), where (the channels) and when (the rhythm). Without a plan, you publish at random, depending on the inspiration of the moment, and you never know what worked.

The central tool is the editorial calendar: a table listing the planned content week by week. A good strategy also thinks about repurposing: a blog article can become several posts, a short video and an extract for the newsletter. Finally, it plans to measure results so you can adjust.

Examples of use cases

  • A 3-month calendar: one blog article per week, reused as LinkedIn posts.
  • Goal “10 quote requests per month”: you publish content that answers the questions future clients ask before buying.
  • An online shop plans its content around the key moments of the year: sales, back-to-school, Christmas.

Vocabulary to know

Editorial line
The style and main themes that make the content recognizable.
Editorial calendar
The publishing schedule.
Target
The group of people you want to reach.
