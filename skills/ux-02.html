Site Map

UX/UI Design UX-02

Detailed description

The site map (information architecture) is the plan of all of a website’s pages and the links between them, like a tree: the trunk is the home page, the big branches are the sections, the small branches are the subpages. It decides the menu and the navigation.

A good site map groups content logically, with labels that visitors understand (not just the company). A common rule is the three-click rule: important information should be reachable in three clicks at most. To build a reliable site map, you can use card sorting: you ask real users to arrange page labels into groups that seem logical to them.

Examples of use cases

  • Home > Services > Website creation / SEO / Design.
  • Reorganizing a site’s menu because visitors cannot find the pricing page.
  • Running a card sorting session with ten people to learn how they group the products of a shop.

Vocabulary to know

Navigation
All the ways of moving around the site (menu, links, breadcrumb).
Section
A big theme grouping several pages.
Card sorting
A method for organizing content with users’ help.
