Wireframe

UX/UI Design UX-03

Detailed description

A wireframe is the skeleton of a page: gray rectangles, lines for text, crosses for images. There is no color, no real photo, no chosen font. Think of an architect’s plan, which you approve before thinking about paint.

It is used to place and prioritize elements: header, menu, title, image, buttons, footer. Its great advantage is speed: you can change it in minutes, which lets you test several ideas. And since there are no colors, the discussion with the client is about the structure rather than personal taste. You can draw it on paper (low fidelity) or in a tool such as Figma or Balsamiq.

Examples of use cases

  • Sketching the home page by hand in 10 minutes before opening it in Figma.
  • Comparing two layouts of the product page to choose the clearer one.
  • Showing the client a black-and-white version so that they approve the organization, not the style.

Vocabulary to know

Low fidelity
A simple sketch without details.
Layout
The way elements are arranged on the page.
Footer
The area at the very bottom of the site (legal notices, contact…).
